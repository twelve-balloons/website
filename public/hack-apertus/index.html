Research & Expertise NavigatorBern Economic Development Agency · Domain: Research & Innovation

Primary objective

Create an AI-powered Research & Expertise Navigator that aggregates these distributed public sources and makes their content accessible through a single natural-language and keyword-based search interface.

Problem

Research institutions generate a considerable amount of valuable information about their people, expertise, research activities, technologies, infrastructures and services. However, this information is distributed across a large and heterogeneous landscape of institutional websites and platforms.

Someone looking for expertise in "glucose sensors", for example, may miss relevant information described as continuous glucose monitoring, biosensing, metabolic monitoring, wearable sensing or CGM.

Datasets
  1. University of Bern web ecosystem — based on the provided inventory of public unibe.ch subdomains (faculties, institutes, departments, research centres, laboratories, services and platforms).
  2. ARTORG Center for Biomedical Engineering Research — website and its associated research, team and project pages.
  3. Diabetes Center Berne — website and its associated research, expertise, team, project and service pages.

The architecture should also be designed so that additional institutional websites and data sources can be added easily later.

Technical preferences
  • Recommended platforms: External environments only — development and deployment must happen entirely outside the Canton of Bern's ICT infrastructure. Use local setups (e.g. Docker) or independent / Swiss Cloud infrastructure. Solutions must be self-contained and run fully air-gapped or on independent cloud instances, with no connection to internal cantonal networks.
  • Specific AI models: Apertus 8B or 70B (v1.5)
Expected outputs

An end-to-end prototype: public web sources → content extraction → semantic enrichment → structured knowledge → natural-language search → source-backed results. Each query should return structured, traceable results:

  • relevant people and contact persons;
  • their functions and affiliations;
  • departments / institutes / research groups;
  • fields of expertise;
  • related projects or activities;
  • relevant infrastructure or services;
  • a short explanation of why each result matches;
  • direct links to the original source pages.
Success criteria
  1. Finds the right people: at least 80% of expected experts/units appear in the top 10 results.
  2. Handles different wording: synonym and lay-term queries (e.g. "glucose sensors" vs. "CGM", "biosensing") find the same experts as the site's own terminology.
  3. Every result is traceable: 100% of results link to a public source page that supports the claim; no invented people, roles or projects.
  4. Structured results: each result fills the required fields (person, role/affiliation, unit, expertise, projects, infrastructure/services, reason for match, source link) where the source provides them.
  5. Covers all three sources: UniBE, ARTORG and Diabetes Center Berne.
  6. Easy to extend: a new institutional website can be added via configuration or a short documented step, without rewriting the pipeline.
Evaluation criteria

Purposeful use of AI · Technical rigour · Value, cost & scalability · Sovereign deployability · Implementation feasibility

Ethics & compliance
  • Transparency & traceability: AI outputs must avoid unverified or hallucinated information. Every result, summary and explanation must be fully traceable and back-linked to the verifiable public source URL (e.g. UniBE, ARTORG).
  • Fair & unbiased representation: the search must ensure fair, objective discoverability across all research groups, institutes and individual researchers, regardless of institutional size or prominence.
  • Accuracy & reliability: the system must clearly say when no reliable, verifiable or sufficient source information is available to answer a query.
  • Legal & compliance: Comply with the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (nDSG) and GDPR where applicable. Only public web content is used; the copyright of source pages stays with the original institutions. Outputs are published as Open Commons (see Terms & Conditions, §6).

Pipeline sketch
1 CrawlPolite crawler per configured site (YAML list of seeds and depth), respect robots.txt, store raw HTML with URL and timestamp.
2 ExtractBoilerplate removal (e.g. trafilatura), then Apertus 8B structured extraction into a fixed JSON schema per page: people, roles, unit, expertise terms, projects, infrastructure. Every field keeps its source URL.
3 EnrichApertus generates synonyms and lay terms per expertise ("CGM" ↔ "continuous glucose monitoring" ↔ "glucose sensors"); dedupe people across pages by name plus unit.
4 IndexHybrid: BM25 on expanded terms plus a locally run open-weights multilingual embedding model (declare its role in the report).
5 QueryApertus expands the query, retrieval returns candidates, Apertus writes the one-line "why this matches" only from the retrieved fields; code checks that every cited URL is in the candidate set.
6 RefuseBelow a score threshold: "No reliable public source found", with the closest units as hints.

Topic classification for Swiss parliamentsOpenParlData · Public Sector / Politics & Democracy (Civic Tech, Open Government Data)

In one summary

OpenParlData.ch aggregates 300,000+ parliamentary affairs and 900,000 documents from 90 Swiss parliaments into one open API, but this multilingual data is only searchable by full-text, with no shared, reusable way to organise it by topic. The challenge is to build an open, Apertus-based classification service that automatically tags affairs with either a standard two-level scheme or user-defined ad-hoc topics, working across all four languages and parliament levels — validated through a real use case with Digital Administration Switzerland (DVS).

Primary objective

Develop an open and reusable classification service for Swiss parliamentary data, based on Apertus and OpenParlData. The service processes parliamentary affairs — titles, texts and associated documents — in German, French, Italian and Romansh, and automatically returns either topics from a standard two-level classification (such as the Année Politique Suisse scheme) or ad-hoc topics defined by users (such as "digitalisation" or "Covid-19"). The approach is validated through a practical use case with Digital Administration Switzerland (DVS), automatically identifying digitalisation-related affairs across the OpenParlData dataset as an alternative to the current manual process.

Datasets and supporting materials
  • OpenParlData API (REST / JSON, public, no key required): api.openparldata.ch/documentation — affairs, documents with extracted text, bodies, persons, votes. Multilingual (de / fr / it / rm), from federal, cantonal and municipal level.
  • Data export: files.openparldata.ch
  • Challenge background & prior work: OpenParlData wiki page on classification (gitlab.com/groups/opendata.ch/openparldatach)
  • BFH prototype: huggingface.co/spaces/rcds/SwissParlTopicModelling
  • Demokratis classification for consultations: github.com/Demokratis-ch/demokratis-ml
  • Earlier classification work from Basel-Stadt (GitHub repos, tbc)
  • Mentorship: Both — technical and domain. Sunday, on the spot.
Technical preferences
  • Preferred languages / frameworks: No restriction. (OpenParlData itself uses Apache Hop.)
  • Recommended platforms: Both — use the Apertus compute & inference infrastructure provided at the hackathon, or run locally.
  • Specific AI models: Apertus v1.5 — 8B or 70B
Expected outputs
  • Source code in a public repository under an open-source licence (README with setup and usage).
  • A working prototype — API endpoint, script, or small demo UI — that classifies affairs from the OpenParlData API.
  • Short evaluation results on a labelled sample.
  • A 5-minute pitch / demo on the final day.
Success criteria
  • Classification quality on a labelled sample (e.g. the DVS "digitalisation" use case; accuracy on the APS topics).
  • Works across languages (at least de / fr / it) and across parliament levels.
  • Ad-hoc topics can be added without retraining.
  • Feasible runtime for 300,000+ affairs.
Evaluation criteria (partner)

Usefulness for the OpenParlData community and public-sector users; technical quality and reproducibility; multilingual performance; innovation; ease of integration into OpenParlData; quality of the pitch.

Ethics & compliance
  • Transparent & explainable results: show why a topic was assigned (e.g. confidence, the relevant text passage).
  • Check for bias between languages, regions and parties.
  • Neutrality: classification must stay neutral and must not judge political positions.
  • Human in the loop: topic labels are suggestions; a human should be able to correct them.
  • Legal & compliance: All data is public (open government data from Swiss parliaments). Solutions should be published under an open-source licence — see the Hack Apertus Terms & Conditions (§6).

Cascade sketch
1 PullBulk export, not page-by-page API calls. Normalise to one record: id, parliament, level, language, title, short text, first N tokens of the main document.
2 DescribeEach topic (APS level 1 and 2, or ad-hoc) gets a short description and 3 to 5 example phrases in each language, generated once by Apertus 70B and reviewed by a human.
3 PrefilterCheap candidate step: keyword plus embedding similarity between affair and topic descriptions, keep the top 3 to 5 topics per affair.
4 ClassifyApertus 8B decides among the candidates, returns label, confidence and the supporting text span (JSON). Only low-confidence cases go to 70B.
5 EvaluateLabelled sample per language: precision, recall and F1 per language and per level, as input for the bias check the C2 page asks for.
6 ServeSmall API: POST /classify with affair id or text, and POST /topics to add an ad-hoc topic. Optional UI with a "correct this label" button.

Deciphering Archives with ApertusUniversity of Bern · Education & Historical Research / Digital Humanities / Machine Learning · Challenge owner @loleg

Main objective

Develop an end-to-end, reproducible data pipeline — powered by Apertus 1.5 — that processes historical Swiss archival documents into AI-ready data assets while maintaining document origin trails.

Your solution should combine automatic document structuring (titling, pagination hygiene, flagging of handwritten/illegible pages), metadata enrichment, and bias-aware preprocessing to create a dataset suitable for training Apertus (or similar language models) on Swiss legal and historical topics.

Evaluate your pipeline against a University of Bern case study, and demonstrate value with a small downstream application — e.g., dossier Q&A with page citations, procedural timeline extraction, or an MCP server. See the slides in the fileshare for a visual example.

Problem

Transforming raw historical archival data into structured, machine-understandable formats while respecting the original document’s provenance and ethical constraints is an important duty of government archives. Current datasets contain partial preprocessing but lack systemic indexing, NLP-ready formatting, and metadata standardization. Frontier lab AI solutions are costly, resource-intensive, and ethically/legally under scrutiny.

Our local advantage: the University of Bern has research outputs that already define a target structure for archivists, so they can serve as a reference against which automated pipelines are evaluated.

Datasets
  1. Stapfer-Enquête: A unique survey of the school situation in the Helvetic Republic, initiated in 1799 by education minister Philipp Albert Stapfer (1766–1840). The ~2,400 handwritten response sheets are held in the Swiss Federal Archives (Archiv der Helvetik, Bestand B0#C.02.3.1). A University of Bern team datafied the survey 2009–2015 and handed it to the Archives, which operate www.stapferenquete.ch. Type/format: handwritten page scans plus the published datafication (transcriptions/structured records). Access: opendata.swiss
  2. Federal Consultations 1960–1991: Metadata of the Federal Chancellery’s online index of consultations. Type/format: structured tabular metadata. Access: opendata.swiss
  3. Archive Database of the Swiss Federal Archives: Metadata of originally 3.84 million dossiers and 257,000 documents, updated in February 2026. Type/format: structured metadata / linked data (RDF) / images. Access: opendata.swiss

All three datasets are published on opendata.swiss — check and respect the licence stated for each dataset, and download from there.

Types of preprocessing that may be needed: transcription normalization and structure extraction; schema harmonization; linked-data handling, entity linking; date/geolocation parsing; anonymization verification.

Technical preferences
  • Preferred languages / frameworks: Python (Pandas, Transformers) preferred for data processing and model interaction, with Gradio/Jupyter/JavaScript for dashboard interfaces. Any cloud platforms (Swiss based / Educational access) and local development environments can be used, as long as they are freely accessible to all team members.
  • Recommended platforms: the Opendata.swiss showcase, e.g. Swiss open data for AI assistants (42 read-only MCP servers over Swiss federal and cantonal data).
  • Specific AI models: Apertus v1.5 — 8B or 70B
  • Model sources: Official Swiss AI Initiative model weights or verified provider API, with required licenses/compliance documentation. Participants may experiment with other LLM variants if relevant for documentation contexts and baseline comparison, and will need to cite all model sources appropriately.
Expected outputs
  • Complete pipeline code repository (GitHub/GitLab) with clear installation instructions
  • Generated AI-ready dataset in specified format (CSV/JSON with metadata)
  • Demonstration notebook showing pipeline execution with sample documents
  • Documentation including API documentation, usage examples, and technical rationale
  • Ethical impact assessment document covering data privacy and bias considerations
Success criteria
  • Data Usability: Pipeline correctly processes 80%+ of documents with <5% structural errors
  • AI Friendliness: Output includes model-ready JSON/TSV with standardized metadata schema
  • Provenance Preservation: Clear documentation of editing decisions and original document references
  • Performance: Pipeline completes within 2 hours for 1000+ page dataset on standard machine spec
  • Interoperability: Output conforms to shared specification ensuring compatibility across submissions
Evaluation criteria
  • Technical depth: Complexity and robustness of data pipeline
  • AI readiness: Quality of output format and metadata for model consumption
  • Ethical compliance: Documentation of privacy preservation, anonymization methods, and bias mitigation
  • Innovation: Novel approaches to handling archival document challenges
  • Documentation & presentation: Clarity and completeness of submission materials
Ethics & compliance
  • Adherence to Swiss data protection laws and archival restrictions.
  • Original document versions should be preserved alongside processed versions if possible. Clear documentation of all data modifications with annotations explaining why changes were made.
  • The Apertus Charter may be used as a reference to include assessment of potential biases in data selection, preprocessing, and model training recommendations.
  • Respect all copyright limitations established by Swiss Federal Archives for public documents vs. restricted access materials. Consider fair use / research exceptions in documentation.
  • Follow all conditions governing access to archival materials (some documents may have restricted access periods), use public bulk open data, avoid crawling and scraping.
  • All work must cite original documents and should contribute positively to open scholarship. Any trained models based on this data should consider appropriate licensing (Apache 2.0 recommended by Apertus)
Mentorship

In addition to a technical challenge owner who will guide you on Open Data and the use of Apertus, two expert mentors from the University of Bern will assist the team in topics like: data science with archival materials; NLP preprocessing and data pipelines; ethical AI and privacy compliance; model fine-tuning with Apertus.

Chapter 4

Apertus in five minutes

Model facts from the Resources & Tools page, plus a rule of thumb for 8B vs 70B.

The model

  • Swiss open LLM from the Swiss AI Initiative (EPFL, ETH Zurich, CSCS). Weights, data pipeline and training recipes are open.
  • Two sizes, 8B and 70B; v1 (base and instruct) and v1.5 (the resources page describes it as multimodal with longer context). The challenge pages point to swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B and -70B on Hugging Face.
  • Technical report terms you may hear: xIELU activation, AdEMAMix optimiser, the "Goldfish objective" against verbatim memorisation, a legally compliant training-data pipeline with a public data-transparency repository.

Use8B70B
Bulk extraction, classification with candidates, normalisationYes, fast and cheapOnly for the uncertain tail

Field note

In a local test with Apertus 8B (v1 instruct, 4-bit MLX, about 24 tokens per second), retrieval found the right passages, but the generation invented details, drifted and got citation numbers wrong. Chapter 4b

Evals from hour one

Each challenge page lists success criteria (chapter 3). This chapter suggests one way to measure them during the two days.

The loop

Eval loop from hour one
1 Gold setQuestions or items with the expected output and its source. Split into dev (tune on it) and held-out (touch once, Sunday night).
2 BaselineThe simplest thing that runs end to end: BM25 only, or 8B with a plain prompt. Its number is the anchor of the whole report.
3 Change one thingRerank, hybrid search, better prompt, 70B for the tail. One change per run.
4 Re-run, logSame script, same gold set, one row per run in a results file: date, change, metrics, tokens, seconds.
5 FreezeFinal run on held-out. Those numbers go on the slide, not the best dev number.

Model as judge: when and how

  • When: only for free text that has no exact answer ("why this matches", titles, summaries). Labels, ids, fields and URLs are checked in code, never by a judge.
  • Who: an open model, e.g. Apertus 70B judging 8B output. Other open-weights models are allowed as helpers; name the role in the report.
  • How: fixed rubric (faithful to the passages? answers the question? right language?), score 1 to 5, temperature 0, the judge never sees which system produced the answer. Pairwise ("A or B better?") is more stable than absolute scores.
  • Calibrate: a human scores 20 answers, the judge scores the same 20. Report the agreement. A judge nobody checked is an opinion.

The minimal harness

Retrieval recall@k for C1, or label F1 for C2, needs no framework. Shape of the script (gateway URL and key from the organisers, model name from their docs):

import csv, json, os, time from openai import OpenAI # works with any OpenAI-style gateway client = OpenAI(base_url=os.environ["GATEWAY_URL"], api_key=os.environ["GATEWAY_KEY"]) gold = list(csv.DictReader(open("eval/gold_dev.csv"))) # query, expected_ids, source hits, toks, secs = 0, 0, 0.0 for row in gold: t = time.time() found = retrieve(row["query"], k=10) # your pipeline, returns ids hits += bool(set(found) & set(row["expected_ids"].split("|"))) secs += time.time() - t print(json.dumps({"run": os.environ.get("RUN", "baseline"), "recall@10": round(hits / len(gold), 3), "sec_per_query": round(secs / len(gold), 2)})) # append to results.jsonl

Traps that sink the numbers
  • Tuning the prompt on the same items that are reported. Held-out exists for this.
  • Gold answers leaking into the prompt or the index (e.g. the DVS list sitting in the corpus).
  • The model judging its own output. Use a different model or a human check.
  • One overall number on imbalanced data. Report macro-F1 and per language.
  • No "none" option. Forcing a label inflates false positives.
  • A cherry-picked demo query that the eval never saw. Show one from the gold set.
Field note

In a second local test with the same questions and the same retrieved passages, Apertus 8B at 4-bit listed questions from the source instead of answering them; at 8-bit it gave the right answer with correct citations. Nothing else changed. A results table across runs makes such differences visible.

  5. "Can someone else run your eval in 15 minutes from the README?"
Chapter 5

Mentor playbook: the 10-minute table visit

A short visit per team: five questions, one decision written down, one follow-up time agreed.

The five questions, in this order

1
"Who uses this, and what do they do differently afterwards?"
Forces a user and a moment. The partner's own example beats an invented persona.
2
"What is the 30-second demo moment?"
If they cannot say it, they have no target. Write it on a sticky note on their screen.
3
"Where exactly does Apertus do something code cannot?"
Jury criterion 1, purposeful use of AI. One step, named.
4
"How will you know it works? Show me 20 test cases."
Jury criterion 2, technical rigour, and the challenge's success criteria. Gold set before features.
5
"What runs if the internet is gone, except the Apertus endpoint?"
Jury criterion 4, sovereign deployability. Find the closed API before Monday.

Architecture calls worth making early

  • One path end to end first, ugly. Then improve the weakest step. Never three half-built components.
  • Structured output everywhere: JSON schema per LLM call, validated, retried once. Free text only in the final sentence the user reads.
  • Cache every model call on disk by input hash. Saves the gateway, makes the demo reproducible and fast.
  • Rules before models: dates, ids, URLs, deduplication in code.
  • Eval script in the repo, one command, prints the metric the challenge names.
  • Scope cut by Sunday 22:00: what is not working end to end by then goes to "next steps" in the report.

Chapter 7

Glossary

Every term in this guide, in one line.

Apertus
The Swiss open LLM (8B, 70B) from the Swiss AI Initiative; open weights, data and recipes.
CSCS
Swiss National Supercomputing Centre; provides Apertus inference to all teams via API.
sprintd
The hackathon platform: registration, challenge matching, team finding, submission.
8B / 70B
Model sizes in billions of parameters; 8B is fast and cheap, 70B is stronger and slower.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: fetch relevant text first, then let the model answer from it.
BM25
Classic keyword ranking; fast, transparent, no model needed.
Embedding
A vector that represents meaning, so similar texts are close even with different words.
Hybrid search
Keyword plus embedding retrieval, combined.
Query expansion
Adding synonyms and related terms to a query before searching ("CGM" for "glucose sensors").
Recall@10
Share of the expected results that appear in the top 10. C1 target: 80%.
Precision / recall / F1
Share of found items that are right / share of right items found / their harmonic mean.
Gold set
Hand-labelled test cases with the expected answer; the basis of every eval number.
Zero-shot classification
Assigning labels the model was never trained on, from label descriptions alone. C2's "no retraining".
Cascade
Cheap filter first, small model next, big model only on hard cases.
LLM-as-judge
Using a model to grade outputs; allowed here with other open-weights models if declared.
Structured output
Model answers in a fixed JSON schema that code can validate.
LoRA
Low-rank adaptation: cheap fine-tuning that trains small add-on matrices instead of the full model.
Air-gapped
Runs with no internet connection at all.
Sovereign deployability
Runs on-prem or on Swiss cloud with no dependency on foreign closed services. Jury criterion 4.
nDSG
Swiss revised Federal Act on Data Protection.
OpenParlData
Open API aggregating data from 90 Swiss parliaments (C2 owner).
APS
Année Politique Suisse: a two-level topic scheme for Swiss politics, C2's standard scheme example.
DVS
Digital Administration Switzerland; C2's validation use case (digitalisation affairs).
Stapfer-Enquête
1799 school survey of the Helvetic Republic, ~2,400 handwritten sheets (C3 dataset).
RDF / linked data
Data as subject-predicate-object triples with global ids, so records link across sources.
Entity linking
Mapping a name in text to a unique id (a person, place or institution).
Provenance
The documented trail from an output back to its original source and every change made on the way.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: a standard way for AI assistants to call tools and data sources. A C3 downstream option.
Goldfish objective
Apertus training trick that reduces verbatim memorisation of training text.
Human in the loop
A person reviews and can correct the model's suggestions. Named in the C2 ethics guidelines.
